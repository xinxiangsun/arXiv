Abstract: The Euclid space mission is dedicated to probing the nature of dark energy and dark matter, through a comprehensive mapping of the Universe's large-scale structure. Central to these cosmological goals are the spectra produced by the Near-Infrared Spectrometer and Photometer (NISP) and reduced by the scientific pipelines of the SIR (Spectral near InfraRed) processing function. Due to the intrinsic complexity of slitless spectroscopic data, scientific observations cannot be self-calibrated by the science pipelines. Consequently, the development of dedicated SIR calibration pipelines, utilising specific pointing sequences and observation fields, is essential. The resulting calibration products are fundamental to ensuring that reduced spectra are characterised within requirements in both wavelength and flux. This paper provides a detailed description of the SIR calibration pipelines responsible for generating the products utilised by the science pipelines for Data Release 1 (DR1). A significant challenge in these calibration tasks arises from the requirement to model the entire focal plane as a unified entity. Such a global modelling approach is critical for maintaining astrometric and spectroscopic continuity across the entire field of view. DR1 provides more than 170 millions validated spectra in both red- and blue-grism modes. The accuracy of the reduced spectra is strictly dependent on the performance of the calibration pipelines. Comprehensive analyses of the calibration products and the resulting science spectra demonstrate that current outputs successfully meet the mission requirements. Nevertheless, specific areas for optimisation have been identified; ongoing development is focused on addressing these critical points to further enhance data quality for future data releases.
